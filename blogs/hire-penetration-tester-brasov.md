Hiring a **penetration tester in Brașov** should feel like hiring a specialist who can prove risk, not like buying a PDF full of scanner output. I am **Petre Radu Cătălin**, a pentester based in Brașov, Romania, and this guide is the same filter I wish every local buyer used before signing a statement of work.

## Start with the business question

Before tools and certifications, answer one question: what decision will this pentest unlock?

Common answers from Brașov teams:

1. We need evidence before an enterprise customer questionnaire.
2. We are shipping a new product surface and want exploitable bugs found early.
3. We want a realistic view of Active Directory or cloud exposure.
4. We are preparing themes related to NIS2-style accountability.

If nobody can name the decision, the engagement will drift.

## Scope that protects both sides

A useful Brașov pentest scope names:

- In-scope hosts, apps, APIs, and environments
- Explicit out-of-scope systems
- Test windows and intensity limits
- Accounts and roles the tester will use
- A named technical contact for critical findings
- Written authorization

Vague scope is how good testers get blocked and how weak testers hide behind "tool limitations."

## What good evidence looks like

Ask for samples of anonymized reporting before you hire. You want:

- Reproduction steps an engineer can follow
- Impact written in business language
- Severity tied to exploitability, not CVSS copy-paste alone
- Clear remediation, not "update the package"

If the sample report is mostly automated plugin text, you are buying a scan with branding.

## Local advantages in Brașov

A **pentester Brașov** buyers can meet (or at least sync with in Romanian timezone) reduces friction:

- Same working hours as engineering teams in Romania
- Optional on-site kickoff for sensitive environments
- Context for local product companies and university-linked startups

Remote delivery still works well. Location matters most for communication and trust.

## Red flags

Walk away, or renegotiate hard, if you hear:

- "We only run tools and send the export"
- No rules of engagement discussion
- Guaranteed "zero criticals" marketing
- No re-test option after fixes
- Refusal to name the actual tester

## A simple hiring checklist

1. Define the decision the report must support.
2. List assets and environments honestly.
3. Ask who will perform the testing day to day.
4. Review a sample report.
5. Confirm critical-finding escalation path.
6. Book a short re-test after remediation.
